Five working weeks a year go to meetings

A senior engineer costs $18,290 a month fully loaded, $219,480 a year, which works out to $105.52 for every hour on the clock across a 2,080 hour year. Some of those hours buy nothing at all. Take one team's standard ceremony ledger: a 15 minute daily standup five days a week, two hours of sprint planning every other week, an hour of backlog grooming, two hours of review and retro every other week. That is 4.25 hours a week across 48 working weeks, 204 hours a year, $21,526 of loaded salary spent in rooms, 25.5 working days, five weeks of the year in chairs.

Meetings bill twice. Every hour in a room costs a slice of the hour after it while the code context loads back up; price that recovery at half and the ledger crosses $32,000 a year, price it at full and it crosses $43,000, 72 percent of the $59,940 yearly plan, spent with no code to show. The calendar also sets the pace of the work: a question waits for the next standup, a blocked card waits for planning, and the queue moves at the speed of the room.
